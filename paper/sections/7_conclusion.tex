\section{Conclusion}
Jev-style candidate decisions provide a clear interface for biological sequence classification: one model receives a sequence, a question, and candidate labels, then returns a distribution whose selected label belongs to that set. Laya-Bio demonstrates this interface on two jointly trained DNA/protein tasks. Its candidate scorer outperforms the implemented fixed-head control in the biological-BPE comparison, while raw candidate input is more accurate than biological BPE. Candidate-order sensitivity shows why valid output must not be confused with stable or correct biological decisions. Comparisons with constrained generation and evaluations of same-modality task and label transfer are the next decisive tests of the interface's wider utility.
