We evaluate supervised adaptation of an open candidate-scoring encoder to short biological sequences, with no additional neural continual pretraining. The study separates predictive quality from token compression and candidate-order reliability. Starting from one Laya checkpoint, we train raw-input and biological byte-pair-encoding (BPE) candidate models, a fixed-class readout control, and a text-only control on DNA promoter detection and seven-class protein structural classification. All four conditions use three seeds and a matched supervision budget; final checkpoints and calibration temperatures are fixed before test evaluation. On a common eligible test subset, the biological-BPE candidate model reaches 90.16\% DNA accuracy and 59.05\% protein accuracy, exceeding the implemented fixed-class control by 1.40 and 7.91 percentage points. However, raw-input candidate scoring is more accurate on both tasks, and biological BPE does not reduce measured training time. Development-set interventions support sequence dependence while exposing candidate-order sensitivity: reordering protein labels changes 9.04\% of biological-BPE predictions despite a small aggregate accuracy change. These results support candidate scoring as a workable supervised interface in this setting, while limiting claims about tokenization benefits, order invariance, and generalization beyond the evaluated subsets.
