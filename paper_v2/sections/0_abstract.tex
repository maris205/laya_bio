\begin{abstract}
Through one interface, a model answers ``Is this DNA sequence a promoter?'' over candidates
\{yes, no\}, ``Which fold does this protein adopt?'' over fold labels, and ``How fit is
this variant?'' over five ordinal score levels: sequence, question and candidates are all
encoded as text, and a single \emph{shared scorer} returns one probability per candidate
--- no task-specific heads, no free-text generation. Such typed-decision interfaces are
being adopted, but have never been compared against conventional task heads under matched
compute. We release \textbf{BioDecisionBench}: 290 leak-proof biological sequence decision
tasks spanning DNA, protein and RNA, unified under four typed decision primitives
(Noul/Choice/Score/multi-Noul), with an admission protocol that preserves legacy blind
tests verbatim, quarantines 1{,}984 conflicting-label groups rather than majority-voting
them, and verifies zero within-task train/test sequence overlap. On this benchmark we run
the first seed-averaged three-way comparison of decision architectures, to our knowledge:
a shared typed-decision scorer, per-task heads on the same encoder, and a frozen small
language model scored by full-candidate likelihood. Averaged over three seeds, per-task
heads match or beat the shared scorer (Noul AUROC $0.629\pm0.050$ vs.\ $0.478\pm0.051$,
disjoint SEMs; Choice and Score at parity), and the shared scorer is markedly less stable
(run-std 0.087 vs.\ 0.030). Its claimed advantages survive only qualitatively: zero-shot
transfer sits at untrained-base level, cross-family transfer is weak ($+0.036\pm0.034$,
classification only), and a fresh task head catches its zero-shot performance with
${\approx}32$ labels. Single-run differences of $\pm0.05$--$0.1$ reverse architectural
conclusions; we document a complete reversal case and propose seed averaging with run-std
reporting as the minimum bar for such comparisons.
\end{abstract}
