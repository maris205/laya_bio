\begin{abstract}
Through one interface, a model answers ``Is this DNA sequence a promoter?'' over candidates
\{yes, no\}, ``Which fold does this protein adopt?'' over fold labels, and ``How fit is
this variant?'' over five ordinal score levels; sequence, question and candidates are
encoded as text and a single \emph{shared scorer} returns one probability per candidate ---
no task-specific heads, no free-text generation. These interfaces are being adopted but never compared against task heads under
matched compute. We release \textbf{BioDecisionBench}: 290 leak-proof biological sequence decision
tasks spanning DNA, protein and RNA under four typed primitives (Noul/Choice/Score/
multi-Noul), with leak-proof admission (legacy blind tests preserved verbatim, 1{,}984
conflicting-label groups quarantined, zero within-task train/test overlap). We run the first seed-averaged comparison of three architectures: shared scorer,
per-task heads on the same encoder, frozen small LM by candidate likelihood. Over three seeds, per-task heads match or beat the shared
scorer (Noul AUROC $0.629\pm0.050$ vs.\ $0.478\pm0.051$, disjoint SEMs; Choice and Score at
parity) and the shared scorer is markedly less stable (run-std 0.087 vs.\ 0.030); its advantages survive only qualitatively (zero-shot at base level, weak
cross-family transfer, caught by a 32-label head). Under a practical
recipe (per-family training on the biology-CPT init) the same benchmark yields
near-ceiling baselines --- 0.627 TAPE fluorescence (92\% of the published ceiling), 0.860
nuclear-protein positioning, 0.785 promoter detection --- showing joint-regime scores measure
interference, not difficulty. Single-run differences of $\pm0.05$--$0.1$
reverse conclusions; we document one reversal and propose $\ge3$-seed averaging with
run-std as the minimum bar.
\end{abstract}
