Given a sequence (GTATTCTCCCCT...) and the question "Is this DNA a promoter?" over candidates {yes, no}, one interface returns P(yes); swap in fold labels for MDFHIRKATNS... or five ordinal score levels for a mutant of MSSSSWLLLS..., and the same model answers — sequence, question and candidates encoded as text, scored by a single shared scorer without task-specific heads or free-text generation. They are adopted but never compared against task heads under matched compute. We release BioDecisionBench: 290 leak-proof sequence decision tasks over DNA, protein and RNA under four typed primitives (Noul/Choice/Score/multi-Noul), with admission that preserves legacy blind tests verbatim, quarantines 1,984 conflicting-label groups, and verifies zero train/test overlap. We run the first seed-averaged comparison of three architectures: shared scorer, per-task heads, frozen small LM. Over three seeds, per-task heads match or beat the shared scorer (Noul AUROC 0.6290.050 vs.\ 0.4780.051, disjoint SEMs; Choice and Score at parity) and the shared scorer is markedly less stable (run-std 0.087 vs.\ 0.030); its advantages survive only qualitatively (zero-shot at base level, weak cross-family transfer, caught by a 32-label head). Under a practical recipe the same benchmark yields near-ceiling baselines — single-task 0.671 TAPE fluorescence (99% of the published ceiling) and 0.845 promoter detection; one unified model 0.860 nuclear-protein positioning — showing joint-regime scores measure interference, not difficulty. Single-run differences of 0.05–0.1 reverse conclusions; we document one and propose 3-seed averaging with run-std as the minimum bar.
